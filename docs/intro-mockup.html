The script, before and after

Critiqued the way a fantasy paperback editor would read a prologue: does each card give one picture, does the voice earn its jokes, does anything make you want to turn the page?

Script critique

  1. It read like an encyclopedia. Dates as captions and summary verbs ("rose", "tore itself apart") told the history. A prologue gives each age one picture: pilgrims kneeling, a legion marching, a legion falling.
  2. The jokes came too early. "Never once asked to be paid" deflated the empire before it felt vast or terrible. Dread first; the wry voice belongs at the end, in Thornwick, where the stakes are small.
  3. The big beat was buried. "The Ember went out" sat mid-paragraph. Now it is the whole first sentence of its card.
  4. There was no thread. Six separate facts. The Third Legion of standing order 14 now runs through four cards and pays off with "the Third Legion stood up".
  5. There was no mystery. The old copy explained everything. The new one leaves two questions on the table: why did the fire die, and who started digging? Both are the real plot (Act I, Act IV), and neither is answered.
  6. Canon was wasted. Standing order 14, the Wickham road, Greyholt and Lord Pellam are real names from the world doc. Real names make a place feel lived in.
  7. The cards were too long for a phone. 40 to 55 words, clauses chained with commas. Now about 35, short sentences, one strong last line each.
  8. The last line had no setup. "You got Emberfall" lands harder after someone important declines to come. Lord Pellam's letter of regret does that.

Art critique

  1. Shapes didn't read. Pilgrims were triangles, the dead were stick marks, the throne was stacked boxes. Silhouette art lives on the outline: a hood, a cloak hem, a spear, a rib cage, a skull with lit eyes.
  2. Depth was flat. Two layers and black. Now four or five, each farther layer paler and closer to the sky, with fog between.
  3. Nothing was lit. Now edges facing the fire carry a rim of light, the fire blooms, and the Ashbound's eyes glow (canon: eye glow reads their rank).
  4. The Fall didn't mirror the Empire. Now it is the same frame after: lights out, bodies down, a banner on one spear still standing, ash falling.
  5. The text box fought the picture. Frames are now full-bleed, composed so the bottom third is black ground for the words.
  6. It was low resolution. Scenes now render at the screen's pixel density.
  7. Motion was aimless. Now each layer drifts at its own rate, the camera leans in, smoke curls, the legion marches in step, rain and ash fall.
  8. There was no finish. Film grain and a vignette tie the frames together, and cards fade through black.

Loading tips

Lore and one-liners, alternating. The lore lines are drafts; the fragment is canon (world doc §7), and the rest go into the world doc before they ship.
