The script, before and after

Revision 2 against revision 3. Read the last line of each card, then the first line of the next: they should sound like one thought.

Script critique

  1. Each card was a closed room. Revision 2 landed a punch at the end of each card, then the next one started cold. Now every leader asks something the next hook answers: "Empires have been built on less." "This one was built on the dead."
  2. The payoffs chain. "Stayed dead" (3) becomes "they stayed down" (4). "Waiting for orders" (4) becomes "got its orders at last" (5). "Until this spring" (4) hands straight to the card dated This spring.
  3. Every card had the same rhythm. Short sentence after short sentence reads like a telegram. Now each card has a shape: a one-line hook, a body with one long sentence against short ones, and a leader.
  4. The eye didn't know where to go. The hook and leader are now set apart (italic, and ember for the leader), and the lines arrive one at a time, 1.5 s apart. The reader gets the hook alone first.
  5. The narrator explained the mystery. "No one has ever said why the fire died" told you there was a secret. Cut. The question now lives in a line the reader can feel: "No one asked what it was burning."
  6. The ending needed a run-up. "Thornwick did what small towns do" sets up the list of small, reasonable failures, so "You got Emberfall" lands as the punchline and the tagline at once.

Music critique

  1. They were gestures, not tracks. Revision 2 played 1 to 3 seconds of sound, then dead air. Every cue now sustains and evolves for as long as its card is up. Rendered offline, each holds a steady level (within 2 dB) for 14 s and keeps going.
  2. It sounded like a renaissance fair. A hurdy-gurdy and plucked strings read as cheerful and medieval. They are gone. In their place: detuned synth pads through a slowly breathing filter, a wordless formant choir, FM bells, and a glass arpeggio.
  3. Nothing moved harmonically. One drone per card. Now the chords turn every 4 to 7 s:
    • Kindling: D minor, B♭, G minor.
    • Empire: B♭, G minor, E♭.
    • Long Dim: A minor, F, D minor, E minor.
    • Now: Phrygian D pulling to E♭.
    • Thornwick: D, G, B minor, A.
  4. It was dry. There was a short room and no tails. Now there is a 6 s dark hall and a dotted echo that dulls on every repeat, which is where "haunting" lives.
  5. The cards cut hard. The cues now crossfade over 1.8 s. The Fall is the one hard cut: silence, a gong, a sub dropping away, then a cold D and E♭ cluster in the wind.
  6. There was no arc. Now the score builds and releases:
    • The Empire has a heartbeat at 75 bpm.
    • Year 301 quickens it to 97 bpm, opens the filter and climbs on a riser for 8 s.
    • Thornwick resolves to D major, warm, and the title screen keeps it.
  7. It wasn't built for phones. Phone speakers drop the low end, so the heartbeat carries overtones, the pads sit in the mids, and a compressor keeps the levels even.

Art critique

  1. Flat fills looked like vector icons. The throne is now lit from one side, with light along each ledge. A per-scene colour grade and a soft paper texture tie every frame together.
  2. Grids gave away the code. The throne's windows were a spreadsheet. Now they are irregular, differ in size and warmth, some are dark, and they flicker.
  3. Circles gave it away too. Smoke puffs and embers showed as discs and rings. Now smoke is soft, stretched plumes lit from below, and embers are hot points with a halo.
  4. The fire was a logo. Three nested flat tongues. Now there are a dozen, blended additively and fading at their tips, around a white-hot core with rays.
  5. The props didn't read.
    • The arch read as a bent pipe. It is now built of stones and breaks off before its keystone, with ivy hanging from it.
    • The mule read as a fish. It now has ears, a gait and a tail, and pulls a cart with a turning, spoked wheel.
    • A block floating in the sky is now a ruined watchtower behind the ridge.
  6. The road cut into the text. A flat grey sheet ran to the bottom of the screen. It is now wet and rutted, its puddles hold the sky and ring with rain, and it fades out before the words.
  7. The moon was two discs. The crescent was made by erasing the sky. It is now clipped properly, with faint earthshine on the dark side.
  8. Clouds were blobs. They are now painted banks, lit from above and drifting at their own depth.

The six cues

  1. Loading: a low open fifth on D in the wind, and a far bell every few seconds.
  2. The Kindling: awe. A D minor pad, a distant "oo" choir, a slow glass figure through the echo, a bell, the fire crackling.
  3. The Empire: majesty with menace. B♭ major, an "ah" choir, a heartbeat for the legion's step, a quicker arpeggio.
  4. The Fall: the cut. A gong and a falling sub, then a cold cluster, wind, and a lonely glass note every three seconds.
  5. The Long Dim: a thin, wavering A minor, rain, a sparse hollow bell.
  6. Year 301: dread. Phrygian D, the choir a half-step apart, the heartbeat faster, the filter opening, a riser.
  7. Thornwick and the title: release. D major, warm, the glass figure turned major, bells.

Loading tips

Lore and one-liners, alternating. The lore lines are drafts; the fragment is canon (world doc §7), and the rest go into the world doc before they ship.

    Still to decide

    1. The script: does the hook-and-leader flow work? Any line you'd change?
    2. The score: is this the right sound? For the real build, the cues can stay live synthesis (tiny, and they adapt) or be recorded as loops.
    3. The art: is this finish enough to build from, or push further (closer camera, more figures, a moving title logo)?
    4. Voice: text only, or should the lines be read aloud later (a recorded narrator)?

    After you sign off: the scenes become a module the game draws the same way (src/cutscene/), timelines go into content/cutscenes/intro.json, and the score becomes a small module that plays each cue by name.
